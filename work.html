W.01

NASA Aspera Mission

Software Developer May 2026 — present University of Arizona

Aspera is a NASA SmallSat built to observe extreme-ultraviolet emission from the hot gas around galaxies — the circumgalactic medium — light that has never been mapped before.

I develop the mission’s geometry and ephemeris tooling with NASA’s SPICE toolkit: generating kernels that compute satellite position, attitude, and ground-track geometry from orbital data.

I also build the attitude and clock kernels for the pointing instrument, converting camera-matrix data into SPICE-native frames so the team can run target-visibility analysis — the answer to “can we see this galaxy right now?” Around 100 researchers work against these products.

SPICE Python Ephemeris Attitude kernels EUV astronomy
W.02

Center for Digital Humanities

Student Developer May 2025 — present University of Arizona

Cultural preservation with a scanner: LIDAR surveys of cemeteries in Curaçao, on-site volumetric recordings, and the WebAR pipelines that make them public.

I build and maintain the center’s Three.js viewers and the AWS S3/CloudFront asset pipeline behind them. The hero of this site runs on the same capture stack — that point cloud is a scan from our stage.

When Niantic shut down 8th Wall, the center’s AR catalog was set to vanish with it. I reverse engineered the proprietary volumetric format and migrated 200+ projects onto university infrastructure. I’ve since taught volumetric-capture and AR workflows to graduate courses, and collaborated with institutions including the Smithsonian.

Three.js Volumetric capture LIDAR Gaussian splatting AWS S3 / CloudFront Reverse engineering
